This is what it costs
to watch someone die
and keep living afterward.

Like grief’s been sleeping with its teeth
pressed into the soft animal parts of me again.

The room’s always wrong afterward.

Too quiet.

Like the walls remember something they weren’t supposed to witness.

And somewhere inside the silence I hear it again—

your body folding against itself like a cathedral collapsing inward under the weight of an angry god.

Bones speaking in unnatural languages.

Your spine arching toward the ceiling as if invisible hooks had found their way beneath your ribs and were trying to reel your soul upward through your mouth.

I remember thinking:

this is what hell must sound like from the inside.

Not fire.

Not screaming.

Just the wet animal music of someone you love becoming unreachable in front of you.

Your eyes had gone somewhere else.

Veins blooming crimson beneath the white like something inside you had already drifted beyond reach.

That terrible vacant shine in them.

Like you were looking at me from someplace impossibly far away.

I kept saying your name like a prayer said hard enough might still shame death into leaving.

Like love could bargain with the mouth of the grave.

Stay.

Stay.

Please stay.

My hands shook so badly they didn’t feel attached to me anymore.

I touched your face

and your skin already felt distant.

Like something the world had begun unliving in secret.

Your breathing turned strange after that.

Thin.

Wet.

Like the dark itself was learning how to speak through you.

Every sound leaving your body felt borrowed from somewhere beneath the world.

Like heaven had misplaced the instructions for keeping you here.

And all around us the night kept opening wider.

Black and starving.

The ceiling above me sagged downward like the inside of a grave.

Like we’d slipped beneath the surface of existence itself and into the jaws of something ancient that had been waiting a very long time for you.

Then suddenly—

silence.

Not peace.

Something worse.

That suspended terrible stillness where reality itself seems to hold its breath before deciding whether to keep you.

And then your eyes opened.

Barely.

Like somebody lighting a match at the bottom of the ocean.

You looked directly at me with all the softness left in this ruined world and asked,

“Why so sad, my love?”

God.

Even now, those words still live inside me like broken glass in a locked room,

catching what little light is left.

Because you didn’t know.

You had no idea I’d just watched death crawl slowly across your skin like ink through holy water.

No idea I was already mourning you while you were still warm in my hands.

No idea something without a name had reached inside me and put out the little light still trying to live there.

Then you disappeared again.

Not dramatically.

That would’ve been kinder.

You kept fading in and out of yourself.

Like something merciless was pulling you backward through the dark.

Your body stayed beside me, but the part I knew had already begun dissolving behind your eyes.

And I remember the moment I realized my hands could not reach wherever you were going.

After that,
the air began to thin.

The walls seemed miles away.

Time split open.

Everything moved too fast and impossibly slow at once.

Shapes leaned over you beneath the failing light.

Hands.
Shadows.
Fragments of voices.

Something unseen kept lowering itself over everything.

Not like a curtain.

Like a verdict.

And I remember realizing,
without understanding how,

that the world had already lost you before anyone said it out loud.

And I stayed beside you with nothing in me useful enough to save you.

Not absent.

Not gone.

Just there.

Present for every second and powerless inside them all.

I watched their hands try to enter a place your body had already sealed shut.

No one said it yet.

The silence knew first.

It settled into the corners.
Into the floor.
Into my mouth.

And as they started to carry you away,

the place went strange.

Your jacket waiting like you might still need it.

Your voice caught somewhere inside the walls.

The bed holding the shape of a body time had already stolen.

Even the glass by the sink looked accused.

Afterward,
I moved like a person whose body had not yet been told it was carrying a ruin.

Everything felt permanent.

Cold.

Unforgiving.

The kind of stillness that doesn’t pass through you, but stays and starts building a home.

My mind kept returning to it.

Your body.

Your eyes.

That sound.

Again.

Again.

Again.

As if disbelief was just grief refusing to become real.

Then the call came.

And whatever I was before that moment closed its eyes and never opened them again.

When they finally said the words,

something inside me went completely still.

Not calm.

Not peace.

The kind of stillness left behind after devastation has burned through everything it came for.

I remember staring ahead while my mind kept refusing to attach itself to what I’d just heard.

As if accepting it would make your death permanent.

As if denial itself was the last thin thread still connecting me to you.

My chest felt hollowed out.

Heavy.

Numb in this unbearable way that made even breathing feel unnatural.

And somewhere nearby,
a voice kept speaking to me,

but it sounded muffled now.

Like I was already trapped inside the first moments of surviving you.

I kept waiting for some impossible reversal.

A sound in the hallway.

A key turning.

One small mercy to prove the world had lied.

But nothing moved toward me.

Not breath.

Not time.

Not you.

Even God felt gone then.

Not silent.

Gone.

And I couldn’t understand how someone could still be warm

and already gone.

How a body could remain after the person inside it had vanished beyond language.

How my hands could hold so much of you

and still have nothing strong enough to bring you back.

Then without warning,

it would return.

Not as memory.

As impact.

A sudden violent awareness that you were no longer anywhere the living could follow.

And my entire body would react before my mind could.

Stomach dropping.

Hands cold.

That sick hollow sensation of reality misaligning itself again.

Like existence had briefly forgotten to keep pretending everything was normal.

That was the true cruelty of it.

Not the moment you died.

The moments afterward where my mind kept discovering it all over again.

And maybe that’s the half-life of you—

how something gone
can keep poisoning the living
with what love refuses to let decay.
